# Continuity Hypothesis

## Introduction

Most theories of information, knowledge and organizational learning attempt to explain how meaning is created, shared and preserved.

The Laws of Persistence propose that an important layer remains insufficiently explored.

The preservation of capability.

This document formalizes the Continuity Hypothesis.

---

# The Classical Hierarchy

The traditional knowledge hierarchy is commonly represented as:

    Data
    ↓
    Information
    ↓
    Knowledge
    ↓
    Wisdom

Within this hierarchy:

- Data represents observable facts.
- Information represents contextualized data.
- Knowledge represents interpreted information.
- Wisdom represents sound judgement.

This hierarchy remains useful and valid.

However, it appears incomplete when evaluated against real-world continuity problems.


# The Limitation

Organizations rarely fail because data disappears.

They rarely fail because information disappears.

They often possess documentation, repositories and knowledge stores.

Yet operational capability can still be lost.

Examples include:

- critical staff departures;
- organizational restructuring;
- institutional memory loss;
- technology migration;
- system failures;
- cognitive degradation.

Knowledge may continue to exist.

Capability may not.
