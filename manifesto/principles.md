# Principles of Persistence

## Introduction

The Principles of Persistence summarize the philosophical foundations underlying the Laws of Persistence.

They establish the assumptions from which the framework emerges.

While the Laws provide operational guidance, the Principles describe the worldview that motivates them.

---

# Principle 1

## Continuity Is More Valuable Than Convenience

Short-term convenience frequently conflicts with long-term continuity.

When such conflicts occur, continuity should be preferred whenever possible.

Capability that survives is more valuable than convenience that disappears.


---

# Principle 2

## Knowledge Must Survive Its Holders

Knowledge that disappears when a person disappears does not truly belong to the organization.

Critical knowledge should survive:

- absence;
- role changes;
- retirement;
- resignation;
- time itself.


---

# Principle 3

## Context Creates Meaning

Information without context loses interpretability.

Context transforms information into something usable.

Whenever context disappears, capability becomes vulnerable.


---

# Principle 4

## Representation Enables Persistence

What cannot be represented cannot be reliably preserved.

Representation transforms hidden knowledge into organizational assets.

Visibility is a prerequisite for governance.


---

# Principle 5

## Recoverability Is More Important Than Storage

The existence of stored information does not guarantee continuity.

Continuity depends upon the ability to reconstruct capability when required.

Recovery must be demonstrable.


---

# Principle 6

## History Has Value

Current state explains where a system is.

History explains how it arrived there.

Organizations learn through preserved history.

Versioning creates organizational memory.


---

# Principle 7

## Hidden Dependencies Create Risk

Dependencies that are not visible cannot be effectively governed.

Knowledge concentration, undocumented assumptions and orphaned systems all increase continuity risk.

Visibility reduces fragility.


---

# Principle 8

## Governance Preserves Trust

Knowledge, configurations and capabilities require ownership and accountability.

Without governance, persistence degrades into accumulation.

Governance transforms persistence into reliability.


---

# Principle 9

## Capability Is More Important Than Knowledge

Knowledge creates understanding.

Capability creates action.

An organization may possess knowledge while lacking the ability to execute.

Capability therefore represents a higher operational objective.


---

# Principle 10

## Continuity Is The Ultimate Objective

The purpose of persistence is not data preservation.

The purpose of persistence is not knowledge preservation.

The purpose of persistence is continuity.

Continuity exists when capability survives change, disruption and the passage of time.

---

# Final Statement

Knowledge creates potential.

Capability creates action.

Continuity preserves capability.
