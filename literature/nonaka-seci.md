# Nonaka and the SECI Model

## Introduction

The work of Ikujiro Nonaka represents one of the most influential contributions to Knowledge Management.

Nonaka proposed that organizational knowledge is created through continuous interaction between tacit knowledge and explicit knowledge.

This interaction became known as the SECI Model.

The model provides an important theoretical foundation for understanding knowledge creation and transfer.


---

# Tacit and Explicit Knowledge

Nonaka distinguishes between two fundamental forms of knowledge.

## Tacit Knowledge

Tacit knowledge exists within individuals.

Examples include:

- experience;
- intuition;
- judgment;
- practical skill;
- mental models.

Tacit knowledge is difficult to document directly.

---

## Explicit Knowledge

Explicit knowledge can be represented and communicated.

Examples include:

- documents;
- diagrams;
- procedures;
- repositories;
- manuals.

Explicit knowledge is generally easier to distribute and preserve.


---

# The SECI Model

The SECI model describes four modes of knowledge conversion.

These modes operate continuously inside organizations.

Knowledge creation emerges through their interaction.


---

## Socialization

Tacit → Tacit

Knowledge is shared directly between individuals.

Examples include:

- observation;
- apprenticeship;
- mentoring;
- collaboration.

Knowledge remains largely implicit.


---

## Externalization

Tacit → Explicit

Knowledge is transformed into representations.

Examples include:

- documentation;
- diagrams;
- models;
- written procedures.

Externalization is often considered the most critical step for organizational learning.


---

## Combination

Explicit → Explicit

Existing knowledge is reorganized and combined.

Examples include:

- reports;
- databases;
- frameworks;
- repositories.

New knowledge emerges from relationships between existing knowledge assets.


---

## Internalization

Explicit → Tacit

Documented knowledge becomes operational knowledge.

Examples include:

- training;
- practice;
- experience;
- execution.

Knowledge becomes embedded within individuals.


---

# Relationship With The Laws of Persistence

The Laws of Persistence strongly align with Nonaka's distinction between tacit and explicit knowledge.

In particular:

- Law 01 addresses implicit knowledge.
- Law 02 addresses representation.
- Law 04 addresses persistence.

The conversion of tacit knowledge into durable representations is a central theme shared by both approaches.


---

# Key Difference

The SECI model focuses primarily on knowledge creation.

The Laws of Persistence focus primarily on capability preservation.

The distinction is important.

An organization may successfully create knowledge while still failing to preserve capability.

Knowledge creation does not automatically guarantee continuity.


---

# Conclusion

The SECI model provides one of the strongest theoretical foundations for understanding how organizational knowledge is created and shared.

The Laws of Persistence build upon this foundation while extending the analysis toward capability and continuity.

Knowledge creation is essential.

Knowledge preservation is essential.

Capability preservation remains the ultimate objective.
