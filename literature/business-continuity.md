# Business Continuity

## Introduction

Business Continuity is the discipline concerned with ensuring that an organization can continue operating during and after disruptive events.

It represents one of the closest bodies of literature to the objectives of the Laws of Persistence.

Both seek continuity.

However, their perspectives differ.

---

# Definition

Business Continuity refers to the ability of an organization to maintain essential operations during adverse conditions.

Examples include:

- infrastructure failures;
- cyberattacks;
- natural disasters;
- supply chain disruption;
- personnel loss.

The objective is operational survival.


---

# Traditional Focus

Business Continuity frameworks typically focus on:

- people;
- processes;
- technology;
- facilities;
- suppliers.

Common activities include:

- business impact analysis;
- continuity planning;
- disaster recovery;
- crisis management;
- risk assessment.

These approaches seek to minimize operational interruption.


---

# Relationship With Disaster Recovery

Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery are often closely related.

Disaster Recovery focuses primarily on:

- systems;
- infrastructure;
- data recovery.

Business Continuity addresses broader operational concerns.

Recovery is one component of continuity.

Continuity extends beyond recovery.


---

# Relationship With The Laws of Persistence

The Laws of Persistence share the same ultimate objective:

Continuity.

However, the scope differs.

Traditional continuity frameworks often assume that capability exists if systems can be recovered.

The Laws of Persistence argue that capability itself must also be preserved.


---

# Continuity Versus Capability

A recovered system does not necessarily imply a recovered capability.

Examples include:

- restored applications with no trained operators;
- recovered documentation without context;
- restored data without governance;
- restored infrastructure without operational knowledge.

Recovery restores assets.

Capability restores action.


---

# The Continuity Hypothesis

The Continuity Hypothesis extends traditional continuity thinking.

Continuity does not emerge directly from infrastructure.

Continuity emerges from preserved capability.

Capability depends upon:

- knowledge;
- representation;
- governance;
- recoverability;
- context.

The relationship can be summarized as:

Knowledge
        ↓
Capability
        ↓
Continuity


---

# Relationship With The Laws

Business Continuity concepts appear throughout the Laws of Persistence.

Examples include:

- Law 00 — Preservation of Continuity
- Law 06 — Validated Recovery
- Law 07 — Living Inventory
- Law 08 — Configuration Governance

These laws collectively support operational continuity.


---

# Key Difference

Business Continuity traditionally asks:

How do we continue operating?

The Laws of Persistence additionally ask:

How do we preserve the capability required to continue operating?

This distinction shifts attention from recovery of assets to preservation of capability.

---

# Conclusion

Business Continuity provides one of the strongest foundations underpinning the Laws of Persistence.

The framework extends continuity thinking by proposing that capability preservation should be treated as a first-class objective.

Continuity exists when capability survives disruption.
