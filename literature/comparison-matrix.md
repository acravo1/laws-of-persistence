# Comparison Matrix

## Purpose

The Laws of Persistence do not emerge in isolation.

They build upon established fields including:

- Information Theory;
- Knowledge Management;
- Organizational Learning;
- Organizational Memory;
- Systems Thinking;
- Business Continuity.

This document identifies the relationship between the Laws of Persistence and existing bodies of knowledge.

---

# Comparative Overview

| Theory | Primary Objective | Unit of Analysis | Final Outcome |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| DIKW | Transformation of meaning | Information | Wisdom |
| Organizational Memory | Retention of organizational knowledge | Organization | Memory |
| SECI Model | Knowledge creation | Individual and organization | Knowledge |
| Learning Organization | Continuous learning | Organization | Adaptation |
| Business Continuity | Operational survival | Organization | Continuity of operations |
| Laws of Persistence | Preservation of capability | Sociotechnical systems | Continuity of capability |

---

# DIKW

## Main Idea

Data becomes Information.

Information becomes Knowledge.

Knowledge becomes Wisdom.

---

## Contribution

Provides a useful hierarchy for understanding increasing levels of meaning.

---

## Limitation

Does not explicitly address:

- execution;
- recoverability;
- continuity.

---

## Persistence Extension

The Laws of Persistence propose:

    Data
    ↓
    Information
    ↓
    Knowledge
    ↓
    Wisdom
    ↓
    Capability
    ↓
    Continuity

---

# Organizational Memory

## Main Idea

Organizations can retain knowledge beyond individual memory.

---

## Contribution

Recognizes the importance of preserving historical knowledge.

---

## Limitation

Focuses primarily on memory rather than capability.

The existence of memory does not guarantee the existence of operational capability.

---

## Persistence Extension

The Laws of Persistence argue that:

Memory preserved
≠
Capability preserved

---

# SECI Model

## Main Idea

Knowledge evolves through interactions between:

- tacit knowledge;
- explicit knowledge.

---

## Contribution

Provides a framework for transforming tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge.

---

## Limitation

Primarily addresses knowledge creation.

Less emphasis is placed on long-term continuity.

---

## Persistence Extension

Law 01 directly builds upon the distinction between tacit and explicit knowledge.

---

# Learning Organization

## Main Idea

Organizations improve by continuously learning.

---

## Contribution

Demonstrates the importance of organizational adaptation.

---

## Limitation

Learning capability is not necessarily continuity capability.

Organizations may learn while still remaining fragile.

---

## Persistence Extension

The Laws of Persistence introduce continuity as a measurable objective beyond learning.

---

# Business Continuity

## Main Idea

Organizations should continue operating during disruption.

---

## Contribution

Provides practical continuity frameworks.

---

## Limitation

Traditionally focuses on:

- operations;
- services;
- infrastructure.

Less attention is often given to context preservation and capability preservation.

---

## Persistence Extension

The Laws of Persistence broaden continuity to include:

- knowledge;
- context;
- capability;
- governance.

---

# Bus Factor

## Main Idea

Knowledge concentrated in too few individuals creates risk.

---

## Contribution

Provides a useful indicator of organizational fragility.

---

## Limitation

Measures risk.

Does not provide a comprehensive framework for eliminating the underlying causes.

---

## Persistence Extension

Law 01 treats excessive knowledge concentration as a continuity risk.

---

# Summary

The Laws of Persistence are not intended to replace existing theories.

They seek to unify them around a common objective:

Preservation of capability.

Knowledge remains important.

Memory remains important.

Learning remains important.

Continuity emerges only when capability survives change.

---

# Proposed Contribution

The primary contribution of the Laws of Persistence can be summarized as:

Knowledge is not the final objective.

Capability is superior to knowledge because it enables action.

Continuity is superior to capability because it preserves action through time.

This leads to the central hypothesis:

The goal is not to preserve knowledge.

The goal is to preserve capability.

The ultimate goal is continuity.
