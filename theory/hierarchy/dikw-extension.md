# Extending the DIKW Hierarchy

## Introduction

The DIKW hierarchy is one of the most widely recognized conceptual models in information and knowledge management.

The hierarchy traditionally describes a progression from Data to Information, Knowledge and Wisdom.

The Laws of Persistence propose that this hierarchy remains useful but incomplete.

Two additional layers are proposed:

- Capability
- Continuity


---

# The Traditional Hierarchy

The traditional DIKW hierarchy is commonly represented as:

Data
        ↓
Information
        ↓
Knowledge
        ↓
Wisdom

The hierarchy describes increasing levels of meaning and understanding.

Each layer builds upon the previous one.


---

# Data

Data consists of raw observations.

Data possesses little meaning by itself.

Examples include:

- values;
- measurements;
- events;
- records.

Data answers:

    What exists?


---

# Information

Information emerges when data is organized and contextualized.

Information provides meaning to observations.

Information answers:

    What happened?


---

# Knowledge

Knowledge results from understanding information.

Knowledge allows interpretation.

Knowledge answers:

    What does it mean?

Knowledge supports decision making.


---

# Wisdom

Wisdom represents informed judgment.

Wisdom combines knowledge, experience and understanding.

Wisdom answers:

    What should we do?

Traditional DIKW models typically stop at this layer.


---

# The Missing Layers

Observing real organizations reveals an important limitation.

An organization may possess:

- information;
- knowledge;
- wisdom;

while still being unable to execute.

Understanding does not guarantee action.

Two additional layers are therefore proposed.


---

# Capability

Capability represents the practical ability to execute an action.

Capability answers:

    Can we do it?

Capability requires more than understanding.

It requires resources, context, governance, infrastructure and operational readiness.


---

# Continuity

Continuity represents the preservation of capability through time and disruption.

Continuity answers:

    Can we continue doing it?

A capability that exists only under ideal conditions does not provide continuity.


---

# The Extended Hierarchy

The Continuity Hypothesis proposes:

Data
        ↓
Information
        ↓
Knowledge
        ↓
Wisdom
        ↓
Capability
        ↓
Continuity

The objective of persistence is not merely to accumulate information.

The objective is to preserve continuity.


---

# Why The Extension Matters

Many continuity failures occur despite the existence of:

- information;
- documentation;
- expertise;
- understanding.

Organizations often understand what should be done.

The failure occurs because capability cannot be reliably executed or preserved.

The distinction explains why action and persistence deserve independent layers.


---

# Relationship With The Laws of Persistence

The Laws of Persistence focus primarily on the upper portion of the hierarchy.

They are concerned with:

- preserving capability;
- preserving continuity;
- reducing continuity risk.

The framework therefore builds upon DIKW while extending its operational scope.


---

# Conclusion

The DIKW hierarchy remains one of the most useful conceptual models in knowledge management.

The Continuity Hypothesis proposes that organizational survival requires two additional layers.

Knowledge explains.

Wisdom guides.

Capability acts.

Continuity preserves action.

---

# Foundational Principle

Understanding is valuable.

Action is essential.

Sustained action is continuity.
