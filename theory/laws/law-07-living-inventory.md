# Law 07 — Living Inventory

## Statement

Everything that exists should be known.

Everything that is known should be locatable.

Everything that is locatable should be recoverable.

---

# Rationale

A system cannot govern what it cannot identify.

A system cannot recover what it cannot locate.

A system cannot maintain continuity for assets whose existence is unknown.

Inventory is therefore a prerequisite for continuity.

---

# Definition

A Living Inventory is a continuously maintained representation of the entities, assets, relationships and capabilities that compose a system.

Unlike static inventories, a Living Inventory reflects current reality.

It evolves together with the system.

---

# Problem

Organizations frequently maintain partial inventories.

Over time:

- new assets are introduced;
- dependencies emerge;
- systems evolve;
- knowledge shifts.

The inventory gradually diverges from reality.

Eventually two systems exist:

- the real system;
- the documented system.

The difference between them becomes continuity debt.

---

# Observable Symptoms

Indicators of inventory degradation include:

- undocumented systems;
- unknown dependencies;
- untracked cloud resources;
- missing owners;
- outdated diagrams;
- forgotten repositories;
- servers with unknown purposes;
- undocumented services.

---

# Inventory Scope

A Living Inventory should include:

- assets;
- systems;
- people;
- services;
- configurations;
- dependencies;
- repositories;
- procedures;
- documents;
- capabilities.

The purpose is not documentation.

The purpose is awareness.

---

# Relationship With Persistence

Persistence depends on visibility.

Visibility depends on inventory.

Anything excluded from inventory becomes progressively harder to:

- govern;
- recover;
- evolve;
- protect.

Inventory creates the foundation upon which continuity can be built.

---

# Relationship With Existing Literature

This law aligns with concepts from:

- Asset Management;
- CMDB practices;
- Enterprise Architecture;
- Configuration Management;
- Knowledge Management.

The Law of Persistence extends these concepts by treating inventory as a continuity mechanism rather than merely an operational tool.

---

# Living Versus Static Inventory

A static inventory describes what existed.

A Living Inventory describes what exists.

Static inventories degrade naturally.

Living inventories incorporate processes that continuously synchronize representation with reality.

---

# Examples

## Infrastructure

An organization knows:

- every server;
- every network;
- every storage system;
- every service.

A Living Inventory exists.

Continuity risk decreases.

---

## Documentation

Every critical document:

- has an owner;
- has a location;
- has a version;
- has a status.

Retrieval becomes predictable.

---

## Knowledge

Critical expertise is indexed.

The organization knows:

- who possesses the knowledge;
- where it is documented;
- how it can be transferred.

Continuity improves.

---

# Relationship With PAPE

The PAPE architecture is fundamentally inventory-centric.

Resources, services, components, configurations, dependencies and capabilities are represented through persistent metadata.

The inventory becomes the authoritative description of operational reality.

This transforms discovery into a continuous process rather than a periodic activity.

---

# Consequences

Without a Living Inventory:

- orphan state accumulates;
- governance degrades;
- recovery becomes uncertain;
- onboarding becomes slower;
- continuity becomes fragile.

---

# Operational Guidance

Inventories should:

- be maintained continuously;
- possess ownership;
- be versioned;
- be auditable;
- support automated discovery whenever possible.

The inventory should be considered a critical operational asset.
