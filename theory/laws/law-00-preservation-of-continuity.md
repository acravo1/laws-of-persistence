# Law 00 — Preservation of Continuity

## Statement

The continuity of capability shall take precedence over short-term convenience.

---

# Rationale

Every system exists to perform one or more functions.

The long-term value of a system is not determined solely by what it knows, contains, documents or stores.

Its true value is determined by its ability to continue performing its intended function over time.

Continuity therefore represents the highest objective of persistence.

---

# Definition

A system exhibits continuity when it preserves the capability to perform its intended activities despite:

- personnel changes;
- technological changes;
- infrastructure failures;
- information loss;
- memory degradation;
- the passage of time.

---

# Scope

This law applies to:

- individuals;
- teams;
- organizations;
- software systems;
- infrastructure;
- intelligent agents;
- autonomous systems;
- institutions.

---

# Implications

Whenever a trade-off exists between:

- short-term efficiency;
- rapid delivery;
- convenience;

and

- long-term continuity;

the continuity-preserving option should be preferred unless explicit justification exists.

---

# Examples

## Documentation

Creating documentation requires effort.

Failing to create documentation increases dependency on memory.

The documented option provides greater continuity.

---

## Recovery Testing

Recovery validation consumes time and resources.

Skipping recovery tests may appear efficient.

Validated recovery provides greater continuity.

---

## Knowledge Transfer

Training additional individuals requires investment.

Maintaining exclusive expertise appears cheaper.

Distributed knowledge provides greater continuity.

---

# Relationship With Other Laws

Law 00 is the governing law from which all other Laws of Persistence derive.

The remaining laws can be viewed as mechanisms for preserving continuity.

Examples:

- Law 01 reduces dependency on individuals.
- Law 04 reduces dependency on memory.
- Law 06 increases recoverability.
- Law 09 preserves historical context.

All contribute to the same objective:

Continuity.

---

See:

---

# Foundational Principle

Knowledge creates potential.

Capability creates action.

Continuity preserves capability.

Therefore:

Continuity represents the highest objective of persistence.
