# Law 10 — Explicit Persistence Debt

## Statement

Any deviation from the Laws of Persistence shall be visible, documented and consciously accepted.

Hidden persistence debt is more dangerous than acknowledged persistence debt.

---

# Rationale

No system is perfect.

No organization possesses unlimited resources.

No team can eliminate every risk.

As a result, every system accumulates debt.

The risk emerges not from the existence of debt itself.

The risk emerges when debt becomes invisible.

Unseen debt accumulates without governance.

Visible debt can be managed.

---

# Definition

Persistence Debt is the difference between the level of persistence required to ensure continuity and the level of persistence that currently exists.

Examples include:

- undocumented knowledge;
- untested recovery procedures;
- missing ownership;
- absent inventories;
- undocumented dependencies;
- missing version history;
- incomplete governance.

---

# Problem

Organizations frequently possess persistence debt without realizing it.

Examples include:

- systems known only by one individual;
- recovery procedures that have never been tested;
- undocumented architectural decisions;
- configurations without ownership;
- orphaned repositories.

The debt remains invisible until continuity is challenged.

---

# Observable Symptoms

Indicators of persistence debt include:

- growing onboarding times;
- recurring dependency on experts;
- manual recovery procedures;
- undocumented workflows;
- shadow systems;
- inconsistent representations;
- unexplained operational behaviour.

---

# Explicit Versus Hidden Debt

## Explicit Debt

The organization knows:

- what is missing;
- why it is missing;
- what risk exists;
- how it will be addressed.

Explicit debt is governable.

---

## Hidden Debt

The organization is unaware of the problem.

Risks remain:

- unmeasured;
- unmanaged;
- unmitigated.

Hidden debt grows silently.

---

# Examples

## Documentation

A critical process is not documented.

The organization recognises the gap and records it.

The debt is explicit.

---

## Recovery

Recovery testing has been postponed.

The decision is documented.

The associated risk is known.

The debt is explicit.

---

## Architecture

An undocumented dependency exists.

Nobody knows it exists.

The dependency becomes hidden debt.

---

# Relationship With Persistence

Persistence requires visibility.

Debt reduces persistence.

Visible debt can be planned.

Invisible debt eventually becomes failure.

---

# Relationship With Existing Literature

This law builds upon concepts from:

- Technical Debt;
- Risk Management;
- Governance;
- Enterprise Architecture;
- Organizational Learning.

The Law of Persistence extends these concepts into the domain of continuity.

Not all debt is technical.

Some debt affects the organization's ability to preserve capability itself.

---

# The Persistence Debt Principle

Every system contains imperfections.

Continuity does not require perfection.

Continuity requires awareness.

The goal is not debt elimination.

The goal is debt visibility.

---

# Persistence Debt Categories

## Knowledge Debt

Knowledge exists but is not documented.

---

## Representation Debt

Reality exists but is not represented.

---

## Recovery Debt

Recovery mechanisms exist but have not been validated.

---

## Governance Debt

Ownership or accountability is missing.

---

## Historical Debt

Version history is incomplete or absent.

---

## Continuity Debt

Critical capabilities cannot be independently reconstructed.

---

# Consequences

Hidden persistence debt leads to:

- fragile systems;
- continuity failures;
- knowledge loss;
- onboarding difficulties;
- increased operational risk.

Debt ignored today becomes disruption tomorrow.

---

# Operational Guidance

Persistence debt should be:

- identified;
- documented;
- classified;
- prioritised;
- reviewed regularly.

Organizations should maintain a visible inventory of known persistence debt.
