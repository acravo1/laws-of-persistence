# Bus Factor

## Overview

The Bus Factor is one of the most commonly referenced indicators of organizational fragility.

Although frequently discussed within software engineering, the concept applies equally to organizations, operational processes, infrastructure and knowledge systems.

For the Laws of Persistence, the Bus Factor represents a visible symptom of continuity risk.

---

# Summary

A low Bus Factor exists whenever critical capability depends upon a small number of individuals.

As dependency increases, continuity risk increases.

The concept provides a practical way to observe the consequences of concentrated knowledge and capability.


---

# Context

Many organizations continue operating successfully despite extensive dependence on specific individuals.

These individuals often possess:

- historical knowledge;
- undocumented procedures;
- operational expertise;
- decision rationale;
- recovery knowledge.

The organization functions.

The continuity risk remains hidden.


---

# The Problem

The Bus Factor highlights a critical dependency.

If the knowledge or capability possessed by a small number of individuals becomes unavailable, organizational capability may degrade significantly.

The resulting disruption can range from inconvenience to complete operational failure.


---

# Observable Examples

Examples include:

- a production environment maintained by a single administrator;
- a critical application understood by one developer;
- a business process known only to one employee;
- recovery procedures understood only by a specialist.

The lower the Bus Factor, the greater the continuity risk.


---

# Interpretation Through The Laws of Persistence

## Law 01 — Elimination of Implicit Knowledge

Knowledge concentrated in individuals typically remains implicit.

This directly increases Bus Factor risk.

---

## Law 02 — Declarative Representation

Representing knowledge reduces dependency on individuals.

Representation increases resilience.


---

## Law 04 — Preference for Persistent Memory

Organizations that depend primarily on human memory often exhibit low Bus Factors.

Persistent memory reduces concentration risk.

---

## Law 07 — Living Inventory

A Living Inventory should identify critical dependencies and capability concentrations before they become continuity threats.


---

# Relationship With The Hidden Knowledge Factory

The Hidden Knowledge Factory frequently develops in environments with low Bus Factors.

Knowledge accumulates within:

- individuals;
- conversations;
- messaging systems;
- undocumented practices.

Capability becomes difficult to separate from its holders.


---

# Relationship With The Continuity Hypothesis

The Bus Factor demonstrates the distinction between knowledge and continuity.

Knowledge may continue to exist within individuals.

Continuity depends on whether capability can survive the loss of those individuals.

The distinction is central to the Continuity Hypothesis.


---

# Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1

Knowledge concentration creates continuity risk.

---

## Lesson 2

Documentation alone is not sufficient.

Capability must also be preserved.

---

## Lesson 3

Continuity improves when capability becomes independent of specific individuals.


---

# Conclusion

The Bus Factor provides a practical indicator of continuity fragility.

It exposes situations in which organizational capability depends upon a small number of individuals.

For the Laws of Persistence, the objective is not merely to increase the Bus Factor.

The objective is to preserve capability regardless of who is present.

---

# Central Observation

Dependency on people is normal.

Dependency on irreplaceable people is a continuity risk.
